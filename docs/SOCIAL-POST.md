# LinkedIn post

Nobody on stage can see the clock, the room, or their own filler words. This weekend at the AI Tinkerers *Agents, Everywhere* hackathon in Seattle, Reed O'Beirne and I built Podium Coach — an agent that doesn't live in a chatbox, it lives on the podium. Your phone shows a countdown and, at most once every fifteen seconds, a single cue of six words or fewer: "Slow down. 174 wpm." It hears you through the Google Meet tab you're already presenting in, and a second phone at the front of the room scores how many people are looking up versus down at their laptops. When you stop, you get a recap: what landed, what to fix, and which thirty seconds the room actually leaned in — with what you were saying at that moment. The rule we held to all day is that code decides *when* to speak and the model only decides *how*. Every threshold — words per minute, fillers per minute, seconds since your last pause — sits in a config file you can open and read, and if a model call fails, a fixed line shows instead, so the phone never shows you an error mid-sentence. Nine seconds into our first live run it told me to slow down. It was right. Built with Deepgram, OpenAI transcription models through OpenRouter, Claude Haiku and Claude Sonnet, and Ambiguous AI coordinating our coding agents across two machines.
